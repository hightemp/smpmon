smpmon 2.1 adds a GPU tab in the style of nvtop.

## Highlights

**GPU tab.** Each card gets a graph of GPU usage and memory use, with its current readings above it: temperature, power and power limit, core and memory clocks, fan, encoder and decoder load, PCIe link and P-state. When power or heat hold the card back, the reason appears in yellow, for example `⚠ power cap`. Below are graphs of temperature, power, clocks, encoder/decoder, PCIe throughput and fan.

**Processes on the GPU.** A new `gpu_procs` table lists graphics and compute processes with their GPU load, video memory, encoder and decoder load, and their CPU, memory, user and command line. It works like the process table: sort, filter, tree, kill and renice, also on remote hosts. NVIDIA processes come from `nvidia-smi pmon`. On Linux, AMD and Intel processes come from the kernel's per-client statistics (amdgpu, i915 and xe).

**Several GPUs.** All cards are shown one below the other until you press `u`. Each press then picks the next card for every GPU widget and graph on the screen, and after the last card you see all of them again. In your own dashboards, `{gpu}` in a graph metric follows that choice, for example `gpu.{gpu}.temp`.

**New metrics.** `gpu.<n>.enc`, `dec`, `pcie_rx`, `pcie_tx`, `pstate` and `throttled` can be graphed, recorded in history, exported to Prometheus and used in alerts, for example `gpu.*.throttled == 1`. History also records the GPU processes.

**Tabs follow the hardware.** A tab with nothing to show on the current host is hidden, such as GPU on a machine without a GPU or Containers without Docker. Tab numbers follow the visible tabs.

## Notes

- The GPU tab on NVIDIA costs about 4% of one CPU core on top of the usual load, because per-process and PCIe data come from extra `nvidia-smi` processes. They run only while the tab is open and stop 10 seconds after you leave it. nvtop reads the same data through NVML, which smpmon can't load without giving up its static binary.
- On Windows, only compute processes are listed. On macOS there is still no GPU data.
- AMD and Intel: encoder load and PCIe throughput are not available, and without root only your own processes are listed. This was tested with recorded data only.
- Remote hosts running smpmon 2.0 still work, but their GPU tab shows only what 2.0 collects: compute processes and no PCIe data.

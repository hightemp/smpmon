smpmon 2.2 draws history graphs where it used to draw meters, the way btop does.

## Highlights

**Cores.** Each core gets a usage graph, its temperature and a temperature graph: `C0 ⣀⣠⣤⣶ 17% 38°C ⣀⣀⣠`. Cores are spread over balanced columns. When space runs out, the temperature graph goes first, then the temperature, and finally the usage graph turns into a meter. The total line shows the CPU temperature and its graph.

**Per-core temperatures.** The new metric `cpu.core.<n>.temp` comes from coretemp on Intel, where the two threads of a core share its sensor, and from k10temp on AMD, per CCD. It is recorded in history, exported to Prometheus as `smpmon_cpu_core_temp_celsius` and can be used in alerts.

**Memory.** Used, available, cached, free and swap each get a history graph. With enough height, each row is a label over a graph of one to three rows, with the percentage on the right; with less height, each row is a single line with the graph in it.

**Disks.** Every filesystem shows read and write graphs under its usage meter. Spare rows hold a mirrored graph of total reads and writes.

**Processes.** The new `cpu_graph` column shows each process's recent CPU use next to Cpu%. It is on by default and is the first column to go when the table is narrow.

## Notes

- Graphs are the default. `style: meters` on `cpu`, `mem` and `disks` brings back the 2.1 look, and widgets switch to meters by themselves when they are too small for graphs.
- The graphs cost little: the Overview tab uses about 0.3% more CPU than in 2.1.
- Per-core temperatures are not available on macOS and Windows yet.
